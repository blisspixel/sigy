# 32-clip local recognition calibration

Run: 2026-09-24 on the development host (Windows 11 x86_64, Ryzen 7 7840U, about 64 GiB RAM). Scope: the full frozen FLEURS calibration partition (4 train clips each for `ar_eg`, `cmn_hans_cn`, `en_us`, `es_419`, `fr_fr`, `hi_in`, `pt_br` and `sw_ke`) plus three generated non-speech controls, recognized by two local profiles. This extends the [three-clip calibration](three-clip-calibration.md). It is calibration evidence for a research harness, not an application feature, a language-support claim, or a platform qualification. Holdout (test) references were not opened.

## Decision basis

The 2026-09-24 decisions in the [language pipeline plan](../../../docs/development/language-pipeline.md#2026-09-24-decisions) authorize pinned, hash-checked native recognition under Windows Job Object bounds, require a measured speech-activity gate, and allow one experiment manifest per acquisition. The clip selection and partitions are the frozen screening selection described in the [language corpus record](../language-corpus/README.md); nothing was reselected after seeing output.

## Execution boundary

The same disposable Job Object harness as the three-clip run, changed only so that the thread count and CPU rate are arguments. Each clip ran one `whisper-cli.exe` process with:

- a process-count limit of 1 and kill-on-close;
- a committed-memory limit of 3 GiB (turbo) or 2 GiB (small);
- a CPU rate limit of 4 cores, with `-t 4 -p 1 --no-gpu -l auto --vad`;
- a cleared environment, output capped at 256 KiB per stream, a 300 s wall deadline, and a check that the job had zero active processes afterward.

No operating-system network denial was enforced; the claim rests on the earlier static import scan of the same runtime build.

Every turbo run exited 0. In the small pass, two Swahili clips reached the 300 s deadline and were killed; both jobs were verified empty. Their receipts show 0.3 and 0.4 CPU seconds per wall second against a 4-core quota, and the Silero VAD pass took 14.7 s and 39.1 s on clips where it usually takes well under 0.3 s. The host was starved during that interval, and the cause was not isolated. The three Swahili clips from that interval were rerun once immediately afterward, completed in 11 to 24 s, and their rerun results are used below. The original deadline receipts are retained in the workspace.

## Results

Metrics follow the three-clip record: the frozen rule (NFC, whitespace folding, case and punctuation preserved) against the raw reference, and a supplementary loose rule (lowercase, punctuation removed) against the FLEURS normalized column. CER counts Unicode scalar values; WER splits on whitespace. Rates are per-language totals: summed edits over summed reference units. For `cmn_hans_cn`, CER is primary; whitespace WER is not meaningful for unspaced text and is omitted, and because the FLEURS normalized column spaces every character, the loose CER compares letters and digits only, with whitespace and punctuation removed from both sides. RTF is wall elapsed time over clip duration, including model load and the language-detection pass. Peak commit is the largest job commit in that group.

`es_419` rows exclude clip 18118144988866964999. Its original FLEURS member is near-silent (peak -88.6 dBFS, RMS -101.1 dBFS), so the VAD correctly emitted nothing and the recognizer reported its default language `en`. Including it would add 177 edits over 177 reference characters and 26 of 26 words, taking both profiles to 27.8% frozen CER and 33.0% frozen WER. This is a data defect in the source clip, not a recognition result.

Across the 31 speech clips, turbo RTF had a mean of 6.36 (median 5.77, range 2.97 to 13.47), and small had a mean of 1.22 (median 1.06, range 0.67 to 4.57). The identical `es_419` rates for the two profiles are a coincidence of totals; the per-clip edit counts differ.

## Findings

- Both profiles produced original-script text for all eight survey languages. Turbo had lower error than small for Arabic, Mandarin, English, French, Portuguese and Swahili, with the largest gaps for Arabic, Mandarin and Swahili. Spanish tied. Turbo's Hindi totals were slightly worse than small's only because of the script substitution below.
- Turbo wrote one Hindi clip (17512382642704834946) entirely in Perso-Arabic script and labeled it `ur`. Without that clip, turbo Hindi frozen CER is 10.4% (44/425). Hindi and Urdu share spoken form, so a script-level substitution is a plausible failure. Language evidence must be able to record this ambiguity, and a detected `ur` on expected Hindi must not be silently accepted as correct text.
- Small wrote Mandarin in Traditional characters on three of four clips; turbo wrote Simplified. Under the no-conversion rule that is counted as error, which accounts for most of small's 41.9% frozen CER. Script choice is a profile property to check, not a normalization to hide.
- Turbo's Mandarin frozen CER (14.0%) is mostly omitted or substituted punctuation; the letters-only CER is 1.3%.
- Swahili was the weakest language for both profiles, with frequent word-boundary errors that inflate WER; one turbo clip (4093223410493050081) had 71 loose character edits over 162.
- Language identification: turbo 30 of 31 speech clips, small 31 of 31. The only turbo miss is the Hindi and Urdu case above.
- The whisper encoder always processes a padded 30 s window, and automatic language detection adds a second encoder pass, so on 7 to 18 s clips the turbo cost is dominated by two fixed encoder passes. Turbo encoder time for the same window varied from 40 s to 101 s across clips, and batch-decode time per token also varied widely. Together with the observed starvation episode, this shows substantial host-side timing variance that these runs do not explain. With four capped threads, turbo ran about 3 to 13 times slower than real time on these clips, and small ran near real time. No capacity or live-use claim follows.
- Peak committed memory stayed under 1.5 GB per job for both profiles.
- A FLEURS calibration clip can be effectively silent. Reference-set hygiene needs a level check before scoring, and a VAD-empty result on expected speech should be reported as no speech, not scored as a language miss.

## Limitations

This is read speech from a benchmark corpus, not broadcast audio. Four clips per language (three usable for Spanish) is a smoke calibration, not qualification; per-language totals over about 120 to 220 reference characters have wide uncertainty. The clips come from the train split, so the speakers may overlap with material the models were trained on; the holdout remains unscored. The metrics use single references and do not account for acceptable spelling, numeral or punctuation variants. Canadian French, Navajo and Klingon are not covered. No operating-system network denial was enforced, and ProcessKit assigns the child to the job after creation. Mapped-file and GPU memory are not bounded by the job commit limit. Timing was measured on a shared, unisolated laptop with the observed starvation episode, so RTF values are indicative only.

## Next

1. Add a reference-level preflight that flags near-silent or clipped clips before scoring, and record the `es_419` defect in the corpus record.
2. Measure a language-hinted profile (`-l` from routing) against auto-detection, to remove the second encoder pass and to test whether the Hindi and Urdu script substitution and the Traditional Mandarin output are controlled.
3. Repeat the timing measurement under controlled conditions (fixed power plan, idle host, repeated runs) before choosing thread counts, then compare an optional Vulkan build as a separate measured profile.
4. Choose a candidate default profile for the supervised recognition worker from these results, keeping the VAD gate, and run the holdout partition only once that choice is frozen.
