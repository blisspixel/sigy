# 0083: Service-owned protected retained readers

Date: 2026-10-03. Status: implemented increment; qualification and verification evidence belong in [active work](../development/progress.md). This is DV-01A, not completion of the interactive DVR or native containment gates.

## Decision

Retained CLI playback reads original media in the library-owning service and decodes a private bounded byte pipe in the client. Both `listen file` and `listen play` use this path. A client receives an immutable specification for exactly one currently retained sealed interval, never authority to open its original file. Service admission, protection and receipt identity commit before a reader starts. Audio output stays client-owned; closing the client does not stop independent captures.

The alternative was service-owned decoding with PCM transport. That would couple a new native execution purpose and PCM lifecycle to this first protection increment. The selected encoded reader reuses the existing private, peer-checked local stream and cooperative blocking-worker supervision. It grants no native execution to the service reader. The configured client decoder remains explicitly user-selected. Creation-time native containment, abrupt-parent-death proof and qualified decoder profiles remain separate work.

The current single catalog owner serializes reader admission with recording deletion and segment release. Reclamation selects, unlinks and commits without an actor await between those steps; admission cannot interleave there. SQL protection predicates and triggers are defense in depth, not a claim that a database transaction makes filesystem deletion atomic. A crash after unlink can leave metadata whose file is missing; fresh playback refuses that input.

## Frozen identity and finite work

Schema v49 stores a request ID, generation 1, recording/source revision, sealed interval ordinal/object/checksum/format/bytes, timeline start/end and file-relative seek/duration, with a versioned specification digest. IPC v50 adds retained-reader operations. Exact request replay returns the stored receipt before fresh retention checks and starts no reader or decoder. A changed recording or seek conflicts. No retry generation, automatic reopen, budget reservation or model operation is added.

At most four unresolved readers and 4,096 lifetime reader receipts are admitted per library. A single object is at most 512 MiB and 30 minutes of decoded audio. The cooperative transfer horizon is 30 minutes; the existing private-pipe accept horizon is five seconds. The reader uses 16 KiB chunks and a two-chunk channel. Inspection returns at most 16 entries, prioritizing unresolved protection. Query, raw field, response and allocation bounds remain enforced. Full-history integrity inspection at library open has a separate one-second, four-million-VM-operation, 10 ms lock-wait profile; interactive reader operations retain their 100 ms profile. The full scan validates every bounded row and digest rather than applying a small-page deadline to all 4,096 receipts. These are selected limits, not measured simultaneous playback capacity.

Admission resolves the exact interval even for a completed single-file recording. A late-start gap gives that interval a nonzero timeline origin. Timeline seek 6 seconds in an interval starting at 5 seconds becomes file seek 1 second, rather than file seek 6 seconds. Gaps, released intervals, open tails, deletion and unsupported bounds refuse before dispatch. Each next object will require its own fresh admission; this increment adds no stitching or prefetch.

## Protection and completion

The supervised blocking closure owns the file and a copy of the library ownership handle. The first pass verifies regular-file size and checksum before sending bytes. It rewinds the same handle for bounded streaming and checks a second checksum, so a 512 MiB object can require up to 1 GiB of file-read work. Channel slots, producer/read/write chunks and OS pipe buffers have distinct memory costs; two channel slots are not total process memory. Existing private-path checks reject links and nonregular files; they are not an atomic no-follow guarantee against another same-privilege process changing paths. The hashes prove observations, not transactional byte immutability; already delivered bytes cannot be retracted after later mutation.

Stop commits `cancelling` before signalling. Existing cancellation and actual close proof grant no fresh authority; their stored update time floors a regressed observed clock at the previous update, preserving immutable admission time. Cancellation, transport failure or a deadline closes the bounded receiver and then waits for the actual blocking reader to finish. Only the worker can construct the exact close receipt consumed by storage. An arbitrary client acknowledgement, decoder exit or elapsed deadline cannot release original-file protection. If completion is unknown or cannot persist, protection remains held and inspectable. A restart changes unresolved rows to `recovery_held`, never automatically resumes or clears them. Held rows continue consuming the four-reader limit. No manual forced-clear operation is introduced.

Blocking filesystem calls cannot be forcibly cancelled by the asynchronous runtime. An unresolved reader can retain the ownership lock beyond the cooperative deadline. That limitation is explicit; there is no bounded blocked-I/O shutdown or cleanup guarantee. A proved original-file close is independent of remaining client buffers, native descendants and audible completion.

A matching finished-worker message ends its in-memory bookkeeping before fallible clock or catalog work. A clock error leaves durable protection unchanged for restart recovery while allowing the actor to observe idle. Stale generations cannot remove another worker. Completion and fallback hold reuse one observed clock; neither failure invents a physical close proof.

## Decoder and client behavior

The decoder pumps input and drains progress concurrently. One absolute execution deadline includes both and child exit, followed by a separately bounded stop/reap grace. Progress parsing caps a line at 4 KiB, a frame at 8 KiB and lifetime output at 8 MiB. Playback frame count derives from the whole wall-work horizon at the declared 100 ms cadence, plus four boundary frames; media progress has its separate requested-duration bound. Verification uses a 500 ms cadence and 64 frames over its 30-second deadline. This avoids both unbounded progress allocation and the old 64 KiB lifetime ceiling rejecting a healthy long listen. Malformed/regressing/conflicting known time observations refuse; unknown keys remain bounded. Reports stay raw. A provisional 100,000 microsecond boundary tolerance is declared before native measurements; short or excessive output refuses rather than being clamped into success.

Output-side seek and duration arguments select the remainder of the admitted interval. Retained input is not paced in real time while discarding its prefix; a system output device supplies output backpressure, while null output can finish quickly. Direct live listening retains its existing input pacing. MPEG-TS stays available for retained HLS recordings without expanding the direct-live format set. The report separates requested timeline range, file-relative bounds, decoder-reported elapsed time, timeline progress and service reader outcome. Progress does not prove frame/sample accuracy or sound heard at a device. Clean bounded output may finish before encoded EOF and close its input; its decoder outcome and reader transfer outcome remain distinct. Input checksum/size failures remain failures even if a decoder emitted samples.

`listen file RECORDING --request REQUEST` provides an explicit replay identity; omission creates an opaque ID. `listen reader show REQUEST`, `list` and `stop REQUEST --generation 1` expose durable state. Read-only inspection works after service exit. Fresh playback and stop require the service. Ctrl+C drops client decoding, requests the exact stop and waits a bounded time for close status; unresolved protection reports the inspection command. Metadata playhead pause/seek is still distinct from controlling an audible player.

The existing MCP playback tool accepts the optional replay ID. Two read-only tools inspect reader receipts; they add no stop or other authority. Backup's canonical media projection includes published unreleased sealed intervals of reserved and retained recordings, so a restart hold on a rolling segment cannot silently preserve a receipt while omitting its media. Open tails remain outside that projection. Actual ownership must be available before offline backup starts.

## Evidence and followup

Acceptance covers reader/reclamation ordering, exact replay, stale generation/specification, faulted cancellation/completion transactions, actual file closure/ownership, bounded queues, checksum and nonregular inputs, clock/reopen/migration rollback, and independent media. Real native fixtures cover prefix-gap seek, raw progress, CLI replay/cancellation, abrupt service death and retained holds alongside the existing capture/playback ladder. Constructed progress and synthetic catalog histories do not qualify long playback or physical recovery.

DV-01B adds exact cited start/end excerpts. DV-01C adds separately admitted segment handoffs, actual player state and gap-aware navigation. EX-02B links station context through immutable identities. [Near-term implementation](../development/near-term-implementation.md#dv-01-and-s-07-protected-playback-before-an-interactive-dvr) keeps those dependencies and the remaining native, aggregate admission and language gates explicit.

The first describes output-side decode/discard behavior; neither establishes measured audible accuracy or forced cancellation of blocked filesystem calls.
