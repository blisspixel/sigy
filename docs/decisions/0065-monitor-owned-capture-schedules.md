# Monitor-owned capture schedules

Date: 2026-09-30. Status: implemented and verified on Windows x86_64. Operational topic quality remains open.

## Decision

A user may opt a monitor version into capture with three finite limits: planned capture seconds per UTC day, planned capture seconds over the monitor's lifetime, and the lifetime sum of maximum recording byte ceilings. These are separate from the existing processing limits, which count admitted recognition audio. An absent capture policy disables future monitor-owned admissions. Existing versions without capture serialize identically, preserving their digests and exact replay.

`monitor create` and `monitor revise` accept the complete set of `--capture-daily-minutes`, `--capture-total-hours` and `--capture-total-mib`. A monitor version remains immutable and a revision still requires the inspected version. Omission of all three flags on a new revision disables owned capture. A proposal cannot change these limits or create a schedule. Paid processing remains off.

`schedule create --monitor MONITOR --monitor-version VERSION` creates one new rule with immutable ownership. It requires the inspected monitor version, an enabled capture policy, and a currently followed source revision from that version's approved sources or candidates. It cannot adopt an existing standalone rule or change the owner of an existing rule. An exact create replay returns the stored ownership even after a later monitor version. Standalone schedule creation, ordinary monitor schedule references, and their independent capture authority retain their behavior.

The common civil scheduler, occurrence table, DVR admission transaction, recording supervisor, shared acquirer, decoder and source grant perform the work. Monitor-owned captures use the same direct finite radio recording path, temporary retention, maximum 900-second duration, 256 MiB per occurrence, logical storage quota and checked source destinations. There is no second scheduler or new agent mutation tool.

## Admission and history

Immediately before an owned occurrence is admitted, the same `IMMEDIATE` transaction checks the current monitor's explicit capture policy and followed source. It admits the DVR plan, marks the occurrence admitted, and records the complete conservative reservation, or commits none of that work. Quota and capture-capacity deferrals do not charge capture allowance. A capture policy refusal keeps the occurrence waiting until the window ends or an explicit policy change permits it.

The full planned duration is charged even for a late start, including its prefix gap. UTC midnight splits that duration into exact integer allocations to the two affected days. The entire maximum byte ceiling is charged before connection. Failed, interrupted, short and cancelled captures retain these reservations. Lifetime usage never refills at midnight or on a new monitor version. Lowering a cap holds future work; raising it requires a user version. Media retention accounting remains independent.

Each admission preserves the occurrence, rule revision, source revision, checked monitor version and digest, action ordinal used to prove followed-source membership, planned interval, duration, byte ceiling and zero cost. A unique contiguous per-monitor ordinal preserves admission order across catalog compaction and backup. Historical policy versions and action ordinals cannot decrease along that sequence. A cited action cannot belong to a later policy version than its admission. Unique occurrence admission prevents repeat ticks, retries and restarts from charging or connecting twice. Admission history and daily allocations cannot be updated or deleted. The current CLI inspection reports ownership, admission policy provenance, separate capture usage and deduplicated refusal counts.

Refusals preserve the denied rule revision, source, full window and limits. Repeating a tick with the same rule revision, monitor version and reason writes nothing. Revising a waiting schedule cannot erase its refusal history or conflate the revised plan with it.

`monitor pause` still pauses processing, including future automatic recognition and translation. It does not pause capture or change independent schedules. Disabling owned capture on a user version holds future owned admissions and leaves already admitted plans intact. Civil-time behavior stays shared: ended windows are missed without backfill, spring-forward slots are missed, fall-back uses the earlier offset once, late admission records a prefix gap, and restart does not replay an admission.

## Verification and limits

Nineteen focused capture fixtures pass. They exercise legacy serialization, exact ownership replay and conflicts, stale versions, approved source policy, independent schedules, late prefix reservations, UTC midnight allocation, shared caps across sources, failure and restart without refunds, lowered limits, policy disablement, processing pause, source removal, durable refusal history, storage quota rollback, SQL immutability, civil misses and the earlier fall-back offset. A genuine populated v39 fixture executes the original migrations and preserves monitor JSON and digests through migration to v40. A conflicting schema fixture proves migration DDL rolls back atomically. A populated schema-v40 catalog checks owned rules, admissions, refusal counts and capture usage through backup, restore and reopen after raised and lowered policy limits and deliberately reversed physical rowids. Hostile catalog edits to unused policies, copied bounds, ownership chronology, waiting plans, refusal provenance or daily allocation dates fail admission or reopen, including a shifted daily allocation whose sum remains unchanged.

The reopen audit verifies policy digests and bounds, followed-source action proofs, original occurrence plans and exact daily partitions. A cited action cannot belong to a policy later than the admission's checked policy, even when its ordinal sequence remains monotonic. A hostile catalog fixture proves that refusal. Policy verification hashes the original stored JSON in its existing domain envelope, preserving historical default-field omissions while rejecting altered policy bytes. Current, ownership, admission and refusal policy references are checked. SQLite window sums use permanent admission ordinals to check cumulative usage against the policy checked at each admission. Immutable policy hashes are computed once per monitor/version during audit. These choices avoid a cumulative sum scan for every historical admission; they do not qualify large-catalog latency.

The existing backup uses `VACUUM INTO`. SQLite documents that vacuuming can change implicit row IDs in tables without an explicit integer primary key, so those physical IDs cannot establish admission order. The backup fixture reverses them and verifies the permanent logical ordinals instead. [SQLite vacuum contract](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_vacuum.html), reviewed 2026-09-30.

The Windows native fixture passes in 28.21 seconds with FFmpeg 9.0.1, loopback WAV bytes and the existing contained stand-in recognizer and translator. It deliberately admits a late owned schedule, preserves its prefix gap, automatically recognizes and translates original French at zero USD, excludes an older independent recording on a followed source, preserves exact replay, captures again while processing is paused without creating a pin, and retains capture usage after disablement. The run exposed and repaired a real eligibility defect: a planned start before monitor creation excluded explicitly owned late captures. Exact same-monitor admission now proves their processing eligibility; independent historical recordings keep their existing exclusion. Processing caps and pause still apply.

One later native attempt failed an existing generic command-success assertion with empty stderr. Its command, stdout and exit status were absent from the old diagnostic, so its cause remains unresolved. Failure diagnostics now preserve those fields and assertions remain unchanged. It did not recur in the subsequent focused run or either final integrated native run. Disposable receipts are in `.agents/monitor-capture/native-runs.json`. `cargo verify` passed 633 tests, including all 101 client unit tests, formatting and warnings-denied Clippy. `cargo verify-media` passed all 15 fixtures, and `cargo verify-coverage` reran all 15 successfully and passed every crate's 80% gate. [Active work](../development/progress.md) records the exact coverage receipt.
